# 3. Curriculum & Levels

## 3.1 Structure

```
Level (0–10)  ──▶  Kingdom on the Adventure Map
  └── Lessons (3–7 min each)                    — one concept each
        └── Steps: Remember? ▶ Watch ▶ Try ▶ Practise ▶ Check
  └── Mini-games                                — replayable skill games
  └── Puzzle sets (themed, adaptive)            — the Puzzle Gym
  └── Bot friend(s) to play                     — apply it in real games
  └── Boss Battle (gate test)                   — mastery check before the next kingdom
  └── Review Island                             — mixed review of everything so far
  └── Certificate + badge
```

- **Placement quest (any age):** a 3-minute, voice-led quest for any child who already plays. It unlocks the right starting level. Earlier levels stay open for review.
- **Mastery gate:** a lesson is mastered when the child gets **at least 4 of 5** check items right (at least 5 items per lesson, including at least 2 different question types, so guessing can't pass). The concept is re-checked 1–3 days later in a "Remember?" warm-up. A kingdom opens after the Boss Battle and the Review Island.
- **Skill sets the content, age sets the voice:** "advanced" lessons unlock by skill, not age. A strong 8-year-old can reach Lucena and Philidor. Every lesson has a Pip version (ages 5–10) and a Nova version (11–15); Little Knights at advanced levels get voice-only notation and picture scoresheets.
- **Approximate strength** is shown only to parents and coaches, never as pressure on the child.
- **"Too easy? Prove it!" ⚡** (any age): any lesson can be completed by passing its check questions straight away.
- **The map moves like the piece:** in Piece Valley the child travels between map stops using the movement of the piece they are learning (rook rails, bishop diagonals, knight lily-pad hops), so navigation is extra practice.
- **Self-guided by default:** every lesson follows the question rhythm and scenario playbook in [document 11](11-self-guided-learning.md). Every rule is traced in the rules coverage matrix (11.9).

## 3.2 The Adventure Map: eleven kingdoms

| Level | Kingdom (map world) | Title earned | Theme | Approx. playing strength* |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **Pip's Playroom** (ages 5–6, offered automatically, skippable) | Ready to Play | Pre-chess skills: touch, directions, counting, colours, turns, feelings | — |
| 1 | **Board Village** | Board Explorer | The board, squares, coordinates, setup | — |
| 2 | **Piece Valley** (6 lands, in teaching order: Tower Town, Diagonal Forest, Royal Palace, King's Keep, Leaping Hills, Pawn Meadows) | Piece Master | How each piece moves and captures | — |
| 3 | **Royal Castle** | Rules Champion | Check, checkmate, stalemate, castling, en passant, promotion | — |
| 4 | **Battle Fields** | First Gamer | Playing a whole game, safe moves, mate in 1, basic mates, opening basics, notation | ~400–700 |
| 5 | **Tactics Jungle** | Tactics Cadet | Forks, pins, skewers, double and discovered attacks, mating patterns | ~700–1000 |
| 6 | **Endgame Mountains** | Endgame Builder | Pawn, rook and minor-piece endgames, opposition, key squares | ~900–1200 |
| 7 | **Opening Harbour** | Opening Navigator | Opening principles, main openings, traps | ~1100–1400 |
| 8 | **Strategy Citadel** | Strategist | Pawn structure, outposts, open files, planning | ~1300–1600 |
| 9 | **Calculation Observatory** | Combination Master | Calculation, visualisation, sacrifices, attack and defence | ~1500–1800 |
| 10 | **Grandmaster Tower** | Tournament Player | Tournament skills, notation, clocks, analysis, psychology, the path to titles | ~1700+ |

\* *Rough equivalents on the Lichess rapid rating scale (other sites' scales differ by several hundred points), for coaches and parents only. Real progress varies widely; these are not promises.*

### Level 0 — Pip's Playroom (Ready to Play)
For ages 5–6, offered automatically and skippable. It makes sure no chess lesson fails because a child can't yet count to 8, tell diagonal from straight, or take turns.

### After Level 10 — Training Plan mode
Ten levels teach the whole map of chess, but strength comes from continued practice. After Level 10 (and from Level 7 for those who want it), **Training Plan mode** gives:

- A weekly plan based on the child's rating and weak concepts (for example: 3 tactics sessions, 1 endgame position, 1 model game, 2 rated games).
- An endless, adaptive puzzle curriculum and an endgame trainer from key positions.
- Annotated model-game series by theme.
- Game import (PGN, and, for 13+, with parent approval, from public online accounts) for review.
- Honest time estimates: typical progress is measured in months and years, not weeks. The app shows progress against the child's own past, never a promise of a title.

### Review Islands (spiral review)
Between kingdoms, a small **Review Island** mixes questions from *all* earlier levels (interleaved practice). Children must visit it before the next kingdom opens. Monthly "Mixed Review" quests keep old skills fresh.

### 3.4.1 Mini-game rules (core games)

Mini-games are labelled "practice games" so children never confuse them with real chess. Where a practice rule differs from real chess, the guide says so.

| Game | Setup | How you win | Practice rules and notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pawn Wars** | Pawns only, on their normal starting squares (or 4–6 pawns each on a mini-board) | First pawn to reach the far side wins | Normal pawn moves and captures. En passant is off until Lesson 3.10, then on. If the player to move has no legal move, it is a tie, just like stalemate in real chess |
| **Star Collector** | One piece plus stars on empty squares | Collect every star | Stars awarded for moves used compared with the shortest route |
| **Knight's Quest** | One knight, target squares, sometimes enemy-guarded squares | Reach every target | Landing on a guarded square sends the knight back to the start |
| **King's Walk** | One king, enemy pieces that don't move, a flag | Reach the flag | Only legal king moves are possible |
| **Hungry Pieces** | One or two pieces and enemy pieces worth points | Most points in N moves | Enemy pieces don't move |
| **Mini-Board Battles** | 5×5 or 6×6 boards with fewer pieces | Checkmate | Real chess rules, without castling |
| **King of the Hill** | Normal setup | Checkmate, or bring your king safely to d4, e4, d5 or e5 | Real chess rules otherwise |
| **Real Board Challenge** | A position shown in the app | Set it up on a real board, play the answer there, then tap the move in the app | Works with any physical set |

## 3.5 Bot personalities (Play Zone)

Bots are tuned Stockfish levels plus "human-like" error profiles, each with a character and voice.

| Bot | Strength | Personality |
|---|---|---|
| Bumble the Bear | Random-ish legal moves; misses captures | Sleepy, cheerful |
| Fizz the Fox | Captures greedily, leaves pieces hanging | Cheeky |
| Rocky the Raccoon | Sees 1-move threats | Mischievous collector |
| Sir Clank the Robot | Solid beginner (~800) | Logical, beeps |
| Luna the Owl | ~1100, good tactics | Wise, gives post-game tips |
| Barnacle the Parrot | ~1400, positional | A pirate's parrot who repeats good ideas |
| Vega the Snow Leopard | Adaptive, ~1600–2000+ | Explains her moves on request |
| Level-matched "Shadow" | Plays at the child's rating ± 50 | Your mirror rival |

Guide characters (Pip, Captain Rook, Coach Nova) are never opponents, so a child's teacher is never also their rival.

**Draw offers to bots:** a bot accepts a draw only when its own engine evaluation is roughly level or worse for it (within about ±0.3 pawns from its side) after move 30, or when the position is dead. Beginner bots (Bumble, Fizz) never accept and explain why: *"Bumble wants to keep playing!"*

## 3.6 Assessment

- **Formative:** every lesson ends with at least 5 check questions in live 3D positions, covering at least 2 question types (see the mastery gate in 3.1).
- **Concept mastery model:** each concept (for example, "knight fork", "castling conditions") has a mastery score.
  The score decays over time and drives spaced-repetition review.
- **Summative:** Boss Battles, plus printable certificates per level.
- **Teacher reports:** a class heat-map of concepts × students.
